01

Two entry paths

Start or continue an investigation

The journey can begin with an open question or resume from an investigation that already contains accumulated context.

Start new

Begin with a new investigative need

A draft investigation creates a dedicated space in which the question can develop.

  • Define the initial question or area of interest.
  • Begin exploring data immediately.
  • Add conversations, participants, layers, insights, knowledge, reports, and workstreams as the investigation develops.
Continue existing

Resume from accumulated context

The user continues without rebuilding previous work.

  • Reopen conversations, findings, layers, and filters.
  • Review participants, knowledge products, and active workstreams.
  • See recent updates and alerts before deciding what to do next.
⇢
Both paths lead to the same investigation workspace.The difference is whether the user begins with a question or with context already accumulated around it.
02

From collections to evidence

Explore and examine the information

The user moves between broad patterns in data collections and close examination of individual intelligence objects.

Explore collections

  • Browse, search, filter, and compare intelligence raw data, insights, and knowledge.
  • Inspect geographic and tabular patterns.
  • Attach complete data collections or filtered subsets to the conversation.
  • Ask the personal assistant to identify relationships, contradictions, gaps, and potential next steps.

Examine individual objects

  • Open an item in a viewer designed for its type.
  • Review content, metadata, provenance, links, and connected evidence.
  • Navigate from the object to related entities, observations, reports, and analytical products.

03

A deliberate choice

Choose how to develop the investigation

The user can continue open exploration, establish a persistent monitoring responsibility, or move naturally between both modes.

Path A

Continue interactive exploration

Use manual analysis and the personal assistant while the question is still evolving.

  • Refine filters and compare evidence.
  • Inspect additional objects.
  • Ask follow-up questions and test alternative explanations.
  • Generate analytical layers and identify gaps.
⇄
Path B

Establish a focused workstream

Turn an indication, hypothesis, analytical object, or unresolved question into a persistent responsibility.

  • Define what must be followed and why.
  • Attach the relevant evidence and context.
  • Assign responsible human or AI participants.
  • Define the developments that require attention.
∞
The paths reinforce each other.Exploration can produce a workstream. A workstream update can send the user back into broader exploration.

05

From understanding to product

Create knowledge and reports

Analytical understanding becomes durable and reusable when it is expressed as a structured investigation product.

Multiple creation modes

  • Created manually by a user.
  • Created by a specialist agent.
  • Drafted by an agent and reviewed by a user.
  • Developed collaboratively by human and AI participants.
  • Produced through exploration or as part of a workstream.

Traceable structure

  • Fields appropriate to the product type.
  • Conclusions, confidence, status, and responsibility.
  • Supporting evidence, sources, and provenance.
  • Referenced records, layers, and related knowledge objects.
  • Review and revision history.

07

Context meets change

Evaluate new intelligence

New information is compared with the investigation’s existing knowledge and the objectives of its active workstreams.

Broad context

General investigation update

The personal assistant identifies meaningful developments, confirmations, contradictions, relationships, gaps, and possible next steps across the investigation.

Focused context

Workstream reassessment

The specialist agent evaluates the new information specifically against the workstream objective and its previous analytical state.

08

Attention, not noise

Create a workstream alert

An alert is created when new information is relevant to a workstream and significant enough to require the user’s attention.

!

A meaningful development has occurred

The alert explains which workstream is affected, what arrived, why it matters, what changed in the assessment, which evidence supports it, and what action is recommended.
